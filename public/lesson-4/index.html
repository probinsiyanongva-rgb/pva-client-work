      What Has to Happen First

      Some steps can't start until something else exists. You can't copy photos into a folder that isn't there yet, and you can't list 12 products before you know which 12.

      Before you start, look at each step and ask: what does this one need first? A folder, a file, an answer from Grace, something from Mark?

      If a step needs Grace's answer, send the question before you start the long parts. Grace's day is your night, so her reply can take hours. Ask first, and her answer arrives while you work.

      Steps that don't need each other can go in either order. You can make the folder first or note the 12 first. There's often more than one good plan.

      Tip

      Habang tulog si Grace, tuloy ang trabaho mo. Kaya itanong mo na agad ang kailangan mong malaman bago simulan ang mahahabang bahagi.

          Will It Fit?

          Before you start, do a rough time check:

          1. Add up rough times for the steps. Minutes or hours is fine.
          2. Work out the time you actually have before the deadline, in your time, after your other work.
          3. If you're waiting for someone's file or answer, count the waiting too.

          The point is to find out early. If it won't fit, you know now, while Grace can still choose what happens. What you do next is one of the calls from Lesson 3.

          How to tell Grace about a delay is in the update and delay guidance in Communication & Professionalism.

          When the Checklist Doesn't Fit

          A checklist covers the usual case. Sometimes what's in front of you isn't the usual case, and following the checklist anyway would mean guessing. That's when you make one of the calls from Lesson 3.

          A small difference isn't a misfit, though. If the checklist still tells you what to do, follow it.

          Several Tasks, One Day

          Most days you'll have more than one task. To decide what comes first, look at four things:

          • Hard deadlines, in your time.
          • What Grace said matters most.
          • Who's waiting: Mark, a customer, or another task.
          • What happens if it's late.

          A quick task with someone waiting can often go first. And when the day doesn't fit, say so early.

              Key Takeaway
              • A step goes after whatever it needs. When an answer blocks a step, ask early.
              • A rough time check is for finding out early, while there's still time to choose.
              • Follow the checklist as written. When it doesn't fit, make the call instead of guessing.
              • When the day doesn't fit, say so early and ask what comes first.
