What's Missing, and Who Knows It

✓ Completed

Your Task Card from Lesson 1 has open questions. Before you do anything about them, it helps to know who has the answer: maybe you already do, maybe it's in a file you can open, or maybe only the client knows.

In this lesson you'll sort open questions that way, and practice spotting when an instruction and the files don't match.

Three Places an Answer Can Be

You just sorted three questions three different ways:

  • The folder name was already in the chat. Re-reading found it.
  • The best sellers were in a file you can open. Looking found it.
  • The Sunday time was nowhere. Only Grace knows her own plan.

Before you put a question on your list for the client, check the places it could already be, in this order:

  1. The whole message, including earlier messages in the same chat or thread
  2. Any files that came with it, attached or linked
  3. The client's shared folders and any process notes
  4. Earlier messages from the client about similar work
  5. A quick search, for general know-how such as what a word means

Some questions will still be left after that. Those are the ones that need the client, and they're worth asking.

Tip

Hanapin muna sa mga file at lumang messages bago magtanong. Kapag wala talaga roon, saka mo ilista para sa client.

"I Don't Know This Yet" or "Nobody Said"?

Sometimes you don't know something because it's new to you. That's unfamiliar: the answer exists, and you can learn it or look it up.

Sometimes you don't know because the instruction leaves a choice open, and nothing you can see settles it. That's unclear, and it usually ends up on your list for the client.

The same request can be either one, depending on what else you have.

In Lesson 3 you'll decide what to do about each question and mismatch. If something does need to go to Grace, the wording comes from Communication & Professionalism, Lesson 3.

Key Takeaway
  • Before you list a question for the client, check whether you already have the answer or can find it.
  • Unfamiliar means you can learn it. Unclear means the instruction leaves a choice open.
  • When the instruction and the files don't match, write down both sources.
