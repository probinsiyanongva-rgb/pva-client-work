The Done Check

Before you say it's done, take out your Task Card and check the finished work against it, part by part:

  • Task: is everything there? Count it.
  • Finished result: the right type, name and place?
  • Sources: the right file, column and version?
  • Deadline: in time, in the client's time zone?
  • Limits: nothing you were told not to do?
  • Open questions: answered, and the answers applied?

Then look for the usual slips: names, numbers, dates, blanks and duplicates.

Check the actual file. It's easy to remember doing something and not notice that it came out wrong, like a folder named for the wrong month.

And leave alone what's fine. Something you didn't expect isn't wrong unless the request or the client's answers say so.

When Grace Asks for Changes

A change request is a new instruction. Read it the way you read the first one, then ask:

  • What exactly changes?
  • What else does it touch? One change often reaches more than one place.
  • Is it a change to this work, or a new task that needs its own Task Card?
  • Which version are you changing? Use the one Grace is working from.
  • Does it change a limit on your Task Card? If so, update the card.

After you make the change, re-check three things: what you changed, what it touched, and what was already right.

Key Takeaway
  • Before you call it done, check the work against your Task Card, part by part.
  • Count, compare names, numbers and dates, and look for blanks and duplicates. Leave alone what's fine.
  • A change is a new instruction: find what it changes and what else it touches.
  • Change the right version, then re-check what changed, what it touched, and what was already right.
