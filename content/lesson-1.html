Every task starts with a message from the client: an email, a chat, sometimes a few messages in a row. Most mistakes happen here, before any work begins, because the message was read quickly.

In this lesson you'll practice reading a client request carefully and writing down what it actually asks for. You'll use the same short note in every lesson of this course.

Most first reads catch the main job. The deadline in your own time, the "don'ts" near the end, and the part that isn't clear are the usual misses. The rest of this lesson is about catching those every time.

Six Things to Write Down

A Task Card is a short note you make before starting any task. It has six parts:

PartWhat to write
TaskEverything you're asked to do
Finished resultWhat will exist when you're done
SourcesThe files or information you'll work from
DeadlineWhen it's due, in Philippine time
LimitsEvery "don't", "only" and "must"
Open questionsAnything the message doesn't tell you

Here's Grace's email again, with each part marked:

Hi Ana! Hope your week's off to a good start. [Background]

We just got back from the spring trade fair and it was so busy. [Background]

We met a lot of new stockists (stores that sell our pieces), and I'd like to follow up with them before they forget us. [Background: this is why she wants it]

Could you put their contacts into our Stockist Tracker sheet? [Task]

The business cards are scanned in the "Trade Fair Cards" folder in Drive, 23 cards. [Sources]

Add each one as a new row with name, shop, city, email and phone. [Finished result]

Some cards have no email; just leave that blank, don't go searching for it. [Limit]

Please don't change the existing rows, [Limit] Mark uses them for deliveries. [Background: the reason for the limit]

Tag the new ones somehow so I can tell they're from the fair. [Task, and an Open question: tag them how?]

I need it before my call with our distributor on Thursday at 10 AM my time. [Deadline]

Thanks! [Background]

And here's the Task Card for it:

PartGrace's trade-fair contacts
TaskAdd the 23 trade-fair stockists to the Stockist Tracker as new rows, and tag them as from the fair
Finished result23 new rows, each with name, shop, city, email (blank if the card has none) and phone, marked as trade-fair contacts. Existing rows unchanged
SourcesThe 23 scanned cards in the "Trade Fair Cards" folder; the Stockist Tracker sheet
DeadlineThursday 10:00 AM Portland time = Friday 1:00 AM Philippine time. So finish on Thursday evening, your time
LimitsDon't change existing rows. Leave missing emails blank. Don't search for missing emails
Open questionsHow should the new rows be tagged: a new column, a note, a colour?

A few things to notice:

  • "Could you…?" is polite, but it's still the task.
  • The limits come near the end, and one comes with a reason attached. The reason helps you understand; the limit is what you follow.
  • "Tag them somehow" is part of the task, but it doesn't say how. That goes under Open questions. You'll decide what to do about open questions in Lessons 2 and 3. For now, you only write them down.

Deadlines in Your Time

When a client says "my time", convert it before you plan anything. Grace is in Portland. In October, Portland is 15 hours behind the Philippines, so add 15 hours to Grace's time to get yours.

Client in…Mid-March to early NovemberEarly November to mid-March
Portland, Los Angeles (Pacific)15 hours behind the Philippines16 hours behind
New York (Eastern)12 hours behind13 hours behind

The difference changes by one hour when US clocks change in March and November. If you're not sure, search for a time-zone converter and check.

Example: Grace's "Thursday 10 AM my time" + 15 hours = Friday 1 AM in the Philippines. You won't want to be working at 1 AM, so plan to finish on Thursday evening.

Some deadlines aren't exact: "EOD", "tonight", "before my call". Write down what you know, and add the missing part to Open questions.

Key Takeaway
  • Before you start, write a Task Card: task, finished result, sources, deadline, limits, open questions.
  • Read to the end, including every message in a thread. Limits and deadlines are often last.
  • Convert the deadline to Philippine time, and note any part that isn't exact.
